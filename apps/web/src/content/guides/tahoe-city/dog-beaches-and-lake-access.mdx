Commons Beach, the main beach in downtown Tahoe City, bans dogs entirely.
Not seasonally, not off-peak. The Tahoe City Public Utility District's rule
allows exactly one exception: leashed dogs passing through on the paved
trail behind the sand.

The map of legal dog water gets specific from there, because four agencies
own pieces of the Tahoe City shoreline: Placer County, the utility district
(TCPUD), California State Parks, and the US Forest Service. Each writes its
own rules, and each enforces them. Everything below comes from the agencies'
own pages. Nothing secondhand.

## Rule zero: the leash law covers everything

Tahoe City is unincorporated Placer County, and county code makes it
unlawful to let any animal run "at large" — defined as off the owner's
premises and not restrained by lead, leash, or adequate enclosure. There's
no in-town exception for beaches or trails. The one designated off-leash
spot is the TCPUD dog park at 375 Grove Street, open dawn to dusk with a
water spigot and cleanup bags.

## The TCPUD shoreline: park yes, sand no

TCPUD manages the in-town beaches, and its pattern is consistent: dogs in
the parks, not on the sand.

**Commons Beach** — no dogs, beyond those leashed pass-throughs on the
paved trail.

**Skylandia Park & Beach** in the Lake Forest neighborhood splits the same
way. TCPUD's rule: "Dogs are allowed on leash in the park but not allowed
on the beach." The park side is a real pine forest running down to the
shore. Woodland trails, a meadow, picnic areas, restrooms, running water at
the group picnic area.

## State Parks: one shoreline exception in town

California State Parks' baseline is strict: dogs in developed areas only
(roads, campgrounds, paved surfaces), on a leash no longer than six feet,
and **not** on beaches or unpaved trails unless a posted exception says
otherwise.

Tahoe City has exactly one posted exception. At Tahoe State Recreation
Area, dogs are allowed at the **Truckee River Outlet** day-use area,
locally "Gatekeepers," along the Lake Tahoe shoreline — leashed and never
unattended. That's the closest legal dog shoreline to the middle of town,
right where the river leaves the lake.

## The Forest Service shoreline: six beaches ban dogs

Around the basin, the Forest Service asks that dogs be leashed at all
times and allows them on most forest trails and much of the shoreline. Six
designated swim beaches ban pets outright: Nevada, Pope, Baldwin, Meeks
Bay, Zephyr Cove, and **William Kent Beach**, a few minutes south of town
on the West Shore — the closest USFS beach to Tahoe City. Rangers patrol
the popular beaches in summer.

## Downstream: the Truckee River Trail

The most consistent legal dog-and-water combination near Tahoe City isn't
the lake at all. It's the Truckee River. The paved Truckee River Trail
follows the river downstream from the 64-Acres trailhead, leashed dogs
explicitly covered by TCPUD's trail rules, with river access points along
the way. The current runs strong in early summer.

## Water safety, per the agencies

Two official warnings. The Forest Service notes that **giardia is present
in Tahoe's lakes and streams**, which rules out the lake as a drinking
source mid-swim. And California's water boards warn that **harmful algal
blooms** can sicken a dog within hours of contact or ingestion; blooms
have been recorded around Lake Tahoe in warm months. The warning signs
are discolored water, surface mats, and posted advisories. The state's
HAB incident map lists whatever is currently flagged.

**From November 1 to May 1, dogs are banned from designated ski trails in
Sierra District state parks whenever snow is present.** The order covers
the trail networks at Burton Creek State Park, the forest directly behind
town, and at Ed Z'berg Sugar Pine Point down the West Shore. The dispersed
forest walking that works all summer is, in ski season, largely off the
table.

What stays open is written down. Two separate groomed trail systems
publish their dog policies, and TCPUD plows most of the paved trail
network.

## Tahoe XC: eight paw-marked kilometers

Tahoe XC is a community-owned cross-country area in the Highlands
neighborhood whose FAQ opens its dog section with "Yes, bring the family
dog!" The grooming covers **8 kilometers of dog-friendly trails, marked
with a paw icon on the trail map** — a designated-trail system that keeps
dogs and fast skate-skiers apart.

The posted rules: leash on in the parking lot and at the trailhead; on
the trails, dogs stay close and under strict voice command; aggressive or
unruly dogs are asked to stay home; no skijoring; only certified service
dogs go inside the lodge. Dogs need their own day ticket, and season
passes exist too — "Many dogs get their own season pass!" per the FAQ.

The trailhead at 925 Country Club Blvd sits in a residential area with
limited parking. Tahoe XC recommends the TART Connect on-demand shuttle
from town, which is free.

## Downtown: the Winter Sports Park

The Tahoe City Winter Sports Park, TCPUD's groomed meadow at 251 N Lake
Blvd, runs four kilometers of classic and skate trails with lake views
and posts designated dog trails under the same terms: leashes in the lot
and at the trailhead, voice control on the snow, free poop bags provided.
The sled hill and ice rink are dog-free zones, and the day lodge is
service-dogs-only. Rentals are on site.

## The everyday walk: the plowed trail network

The TCPUD paved trail system is winter's workhorse. The district plows
**16 of its 23 trail miles** on a prioritized schedule, starting once
three inches fall, so the Truckee River Trail out of 64-Acres Park and
stretches of the lakefront path stay walkable in almost any conditions.
TCPUD's short-leash rule applies year-round. The bikes are gone in
winter; the snowplows aren't.

## The winter stays

Both in-town verified stays operate through winter. Basecamp Tahoe City
($40 flat pet fee) is a short walk from the Winter Sports Park; its hard
rule is that pets are never left alone in the room. Mother Nature's Inn
($75 per stay, two-pet max) sits between the lakefront and the golf
course, minutes from Palisades and Homewood. On the West Shore, Sunnyside
takes dogs in pet-designated rooms ($60 per stay), right on the plowed
West Shore Trail.

## Cold-weather dog care, from the agencies

The Forest Service's Tahoe-basin dog guidance is written for exactly this
season: cold, altitude, snow exhaustion, and ice-ball buildup between the
pads — the snowball problem that ends outings early. Musher's wax or
booties solve it, and Pet Station on West Lake Blvd runs a self-serve dog
wash for the aftermath.

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers owns this ground, and it puts the rule in one
line on its Martis Creek Lake page, among the things it asks visitors to do:
"Keeping your pets on a leash at all times." No seasonal exception. No
off-leash zone, and no split between the trail and the meadow. The federal
regulation behind it, 36 CFR 327.11(a), sets the length at "a leash under six
feet in length," requires owners to pick up after their animals, and exempts
service animals trained to assist people with disabilities. Corps park rangers
patrol the area alongside county sheriff deputies, and the project rules are
posted throughout the facility.

The wildlife area is on the west side of Highway 267, south of the Truckee
Tahoe Airport, and the Corps describes a 4.3-mile loop for hiking and biking
that runs along Martis Creek through conifer forest and open meadow. Wooden
boardwalk carries the tread over the wet ground in the middle of the valley;
it's the first thing that tells you you've left the forest section. The Corps
lists the site as number 37 in the California Wildlife Viewing Guide. Mule
deer, black bear, bald eagles, osprey, mountain bluebirds, waterfowl.

Those boardwalk stretches belong to the Tompkins Memorial Trail, which
Northstar Community Services District maintains as part of a 17-plus-mile
network built with the Corps and Northstar California Resort. NCSD calls it
open year-round. The district publishes nothing about dogs either way, so the
Corps rule is the one that governs.

The meadow itself is a construction site of a particular kind. The Truckee
River Watershed Council, working with the Corps, NCSD, and Northstar, is
restoring 70 acres of wet meadow and two miles of stream, and says the ground
will stay fragile through revegetation.

The Corps opens the project the last Saturday in April and closes it November
15. Facilities shut for winter, though cross-country skiing and snowshoeing
carry on with parking restricted. Its published fee list covers camping and
special use; no day-use fee appears for the wildlife area, and nothing is
published about drinking water at the trailhead. Across town, East Beach at
Donner Memorial State Park runs on the same six-foot leash.

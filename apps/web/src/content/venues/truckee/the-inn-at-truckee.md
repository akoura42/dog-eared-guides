The Inn at Truckee publishes its dog policy in one clause, tacked onto a
paragraph about breakfast and clean rooms: "In addition, we are one of the only
pet-friendly hotels in Truckee!" That's it. That is the entire published
policy.

So the gaps are wide, and they're real gaps. The site names no fee, no cap on
the number of dogs, no size or breed cutoff, and no rule about where on the
property a dog can be. Booking sites carry pet fees for this property that
disagree with each other: fifteen dollars a night on one, forty per pet per
night on another, forty-five on a third. Those are distribution-feed strings,
not the inn's words. BringFido's row goes further and says dogs aren't allowed
here at all, which the inn's own homepage contradicts outright.

The address is 11506 Deerfield Drive, in the Gateway commercial pocket off
Donner Pass Road, about a mile and a half west of Commercial Row and downtown.
The site says dining sits within walking distance. Donner Memorial State Park
is a little over a mile further west along Donner Pass Road, and East Beach,
the stretch of Donner Lake shore that a state park order names as open to
leashed dogs, is roughly a mile and a half from the door.

Off the property, the town standard governs. Truckee prohibits dogs at large,
and Municipal Code Title 8 defines the alternative to a leash: "voice and
visual control requires that the owner is in the immediate presence of the
animal and can demonstrate effective control of the animal." That latitude
stops at the state park boundary, where the posted rule is a six-foot leash,
year-round. The inn publishes nothing about leashes on its own lot.

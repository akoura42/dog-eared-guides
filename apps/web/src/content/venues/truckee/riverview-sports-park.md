The rule that names this park is not in the town code. It's in the recreation
district's field policy, #601, approved April 2018: "All animals must be on a
leash and in its owner's control at TDRPD athletic fields in Regional,
Riverview, Meadow and Glenshire parks, consistent with Truckee Municipal Code
8.01.420." Same paragraph, next sentence: owners clean up waste and carry
responsibility for damage or injuries.

That leash line attaches to the athletic fields. Riverview has five of them,
plus a regulation baseball field with no lights. Off the fields, the town's
ordinary standard governs, and it isn't a leash law. Sec. 8.01.020(f) counts an
animal as controlled by "visual and voice control" or by a leash, and the
seasonal leash carve-out in Sec. 8.01.420(d) names the Truckee River Regional
Park only. Riverview isn't in it.

The Legacy Trail's western end runs through the property. The district's trail
page asks visitors to "keep your dogs on a leash and pick up after them." The
town's trail etiquette flyer words it differently — "Keep dogs under control;
carry a leash to use if needed" — and notes pet waste stations and trash
receptacles along the route. The trail is paved from the Regional Park to
Glenshire, about a mile and a half west of here, and the town clears it once a
day after snow.

Then the bike park. A 30,000-square-foot pump and strider track, upper and
lower dirt jump zones, slopestyle and dual slalom lines, a 1.5-mile XC loop.
Sunrise to sunset, seven days, summer months, weather permitting. Riders come
over the rollers fast and blind. No published dog rule covers that ground from
either the district or the nonprofit that built it.

Restrooms, parking, grills, picnic tables, and a covered shelter that rents on
limited availability. Bocce courts open to the public. The district posts no
hours for the park as a whole and nothing about drinking water.

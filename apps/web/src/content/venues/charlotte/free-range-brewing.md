Free Range Brewing publishes a dog policy on its own site, under a heading that
says so: "We love and welcome well-behaved pups! To keep a safe environment to
roam in, we do ask that they stay on leashes and out of high-traffic areas."
That's the whole of it. Leashes, and clearance from wherever people are moving.
The same sentence runs on the page for the company's other location at Camp
North End, and neither page separates the taproom from the patio. Which areas
count as high-traffic isn't spelled out. Water bowls go unmentioned across the
entire site.

The room is roughly 1,800 square feet of what the brewery calls "a refined
industrial space with a laid back atmosphere": a stage, barrel high-tops, beer
garden tables, aging barrels along the walls. Glass roll-up doors make up one
side. Open, they put the taproom and the outside patio on the same floor, which
is the detail that decides what the space is like on any given afternoon.

Parking sits on the property. There's also free parallel parking along E 27th
Street and a garage on the left side of the E 27th Street entrance.

Hours as posted: Sunday 11 a.m. to 8 p.m., Monday through Thursday 3 to 10,
Friday and Saturday 11 to 11.

Food comes from Vivienne, a Nordic-inspired kitchen operating on site
Wednesday through Sunday, with Danish-style røde pølser and Faroese seafood on
the board. Kitchen hours run 4 to 9 Wednesday through Friday, 11 to 9 Saturday,
11 to 6 Sunday. Outside food is allowed when the kitchen is closed. The menus
are posted as images rather than text, so no dish list is captured in this
listing.

Beer is house-brewed and canned on site, with a to-go fridge at the front. The
brewery puts the local-sourcing figure at about 95 percent of ingredients
across everything it makes.

NoDa Brewing Company is about a mile north on W 32nd. Birdsong Brewing is down
N Davidson the other way, toward Optimist Park.

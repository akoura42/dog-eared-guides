Shuffletown Dog Park sits at the east end of Shuffletown Park, a 54-acre
Mecklenburg County park on Bellhaven Boulevard roughly eight miles northwest of
Uptown. The county's dog parks page is where the rules come from, and they're
written for the whole system rather than for this site.

They're short. County dog parks are "open daily from 7:30 a.m. to sunset," so
closing slides through the year. Vaccinations are a condition of entry, in the
county's words "Rabies with Tag Number, DHLPP & Bordetella," required to use
the off-leash areas. No children under the age of 12 are allowed. Owners "must
follow all posted dog park rules," which puts the signage at the gate above
anything published online.

The county's per-park page for Shuffletown Dog Park loads as an empty template:
a heading over a map placeholder, nothing under it. So the specifics stay blank
here. OpenStreetMap maps two adjoining enclosures, and directories describe a
large-dog side and a small-dog side, but the county publishes neither the split
nor the fenced acreage nor anything about water or a fee.

Outside the fence, the ordinance governs. Section 26 reads, "It shall be
unlawful to have pets in the areas of County parks where pets are expressly
prohibited," which leaves dogs allowed by default on the grounds. No leash rule
is published for the park, so the county animal ordinance applies: physical
restraint anywhere off your own property. Section 11 bars bathing dogs in park
water features. Section 31 makes staying past the posted closing time unlawful,
and that time appears nowhere online.

The park is the old Shuffletown Dragway, a drag strip that opened in 1958. Pete
Beeman's sculpture "Shuffletown Hand" stands about five hundred feet west of
the dog park fence.

Construction is live elsewhere on the property. Volunteers built a replacement
playground on July 30, 2026, and a sprayground, skate park, and basketball
court are proposed near the sand volleyball courts and the parking lot. The dog
park isn't in that scope.

(980) 314-1001 rings the North Region parks office, not a phone on site.

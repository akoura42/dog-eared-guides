Davis Flohr Park is a one-acre corner lot in NoDa, a few doors off Matheson
Avenue. Mecklenburg County owns it and lists it as a Neighborhood park in its
own GIS layer. Dogs are allowed. The county's parks ordinance handles pets by
exception rather than by permission: "It shall be unlawful to have pets in the
areas of County parks where pets are expressly prohibited." Nothing the county
publishes prohibits them here, and there's no per-park page to say otherwise.

Leash policy isn't published for this park. That leaves the county animal
ordinance, Sec. 3-71, as the operative rule: physical restraint anywhere off
your own property. Water bowls and fountains go unmentioned in anything
official. There are no restrooms.

The park was Clemson Avenue Park until November 17, 2020. The county renamed it
for Davis Flohr, a NoDa thirteen-year-old who died in 2019, after a year of
petitioning by a family friend. A homemade orange sign reading "Live Like
Davis" stands in the park, and it's the first thing that marks this place as
different from any other pocket park in the neighborhood.

The rest is small and open, and visible in one glance from the sidewalk: a swing
set, newer playground equipment with climbing structures, a stationary adult
cardio station, a basketball half-court with a single goal, benches, and a
paved path around a patch of field. Shade comes from perimeter trees rather than
anything over the play area.

Parking is on-street only. No lot, no gate.

Hours aren't published anywhere official. OpenStreetMap records the park as
sunrise to sunset and a directory listing puts it at 7 a.m. to 7 p.m., neither
of which is the county speaking. Sec. 31 of the ordinance points at the posted
closing time, and that sign is on site.

Heist Brewery sits about half a mile northwest on North Davidson. Divine Barrel
Brewing is under a mile north.

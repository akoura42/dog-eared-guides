Colonial Park is a Mecklenburg County neighborhood park at 219 Providence
Road, in Crescent Heights, the wedge of old bungalows between Elizabeth and
Eastover. The county's parks ordinance is the document that governs dogs here.
Section 26 runs one sentence: "It shall be unlawful to have pets in the areas
of County parks where pets are expressly prohibited." Dogs are allowed by
default. A posted sign is the only thing that narrows that.

There is a playground, and playground signage is where such prohibitions
usually turn up. Nothing specific to Colonial Park appears online either way,
because the county retired its per-park pages in the parkandrec.mecknc.gov
redesign and the old Colonial page now 404s. Leash rules for the park aren't
published. County animal ordinance Sec. 3-71 requires physical restraint off
your own property, so that's the operative rule on the grounds.

The shape is a leftover from 1907. Crescent Heights was Charlotte's first
break with the street grid, and Thomas Hanchett's neighborhood history for the
Charlotte-Mecklenburg Historic Properties Commission describes the park as it
was drawn: "a half-moon shaped greensward named Colonial Park, bounded by
Circle Avenue and Providence Road." It got smaller. Elizabeth Realty ran Chase,
Alberto, and Phil Aull streets through part of it to sell house lots, and those
streets are still there. What's left is a crescent of lawn with bites out of it.

One edge is Providence Road, which is NC 16 and carries arterial traffic. The
Circle Avenue side is residential and runs quieter. Benches, waste baskets, and
two bike racks sit along the concrete walks, and parking is a small lot off the
service drive. A drinking fountain is mapped near the playground; the last
OpenStreetMap survey, in July 2025, recorded it as not working.

Little Sugar Creek Greenway is about three-quarters of a mile west, Freedom
Park a mile and a half southwest.

The county's own park record leaves the size field blank, so there's no
official acreage. The posted closing time is on a sign at the park and nowhere
online, and (980) 314-1002 rings the Central Region parks office rather than
anything at the site.

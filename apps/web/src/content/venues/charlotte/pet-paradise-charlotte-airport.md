Pet Paradise runs its Charlotte location as a boarding and day camp resort on
Boyer Street, in the industrial pocket northeast of Charlotte Douglas. Dogs are
the clientele here, indoors and out. Admission runs on vaccine records rather
than an interview: the company requires "Rabies, DAPP, Bordetella," and states
plainly that "Pet Paradise does not require a temperament test or interview
prior to boarding."

The vaccine rule has no give in it. "Waiver letters regarding vaccines, even
from a veterinarian, are not accepted. Titer letters are acceptable for
Distemper only." Puppies come in at twelve weeks with two DAPP rounds behind
them. Canine influenza is encouraged and not required.

Two published rules narrow who plays with whom. Unaltered dogs past six months
come out of the group entirely: "Once a puppy reaches 6 months of age or
develops maturity, and they are unaltered, they will only be allowed play
sessions with a Pet Paradise Playtime Coordinator or with other dogs from their
family." The exclusion list names "Extreme dog aggression or people aggression."
Everything else gets sorted on arrival by size, temperament, age and breed. No
breed list is published, and no weight cap gates entry.

Weight does decide the room. Tiny Town Cabana is "a cozy space specifically
designed for guests under 30 pounds," with its own indoor climate-controlled
play yard and a 24/7 webcam. The patio suites open onto private outdoor patios;
the yards those lead to hold "a bone-shaped swimming pool and splash area."

Hours run 6:30 a.m. to 7 p.m. weekdays, 7 to 6 on weekends. Early drop-off
between 4 and 6 a.m. costs twenty dollars. Anything outside the posted windows
bills as another night unless it's booked 48 hours ahead.

Bedding comes with the suite: "We provide all guests with premium bedding and
blanket at no additional charge." The matching rule is that owners "do not
bring any bedding, fabrics or plush toys," and no rawhides.

One thing directories get wrong. The Lake Norman location in Huntersville has a
NewDay Veterinary Care clinic on site; this one doesn't, and NewDay's own
location list carries no Charlotte entry.

Water bowls aren't published one way or the other. No leash rule for the lobby
or the lot appears either, which leaves Mecklenburg County's animal ordinance
(Sec. 3-71) and its physical-restraint requirement as the rule between the car
and the door. Aloft Charlotte Airport is about three miles south.

Druid Hills Park is a Mecklenburg County neighborhood park at 2801 Poinsett
Street, roughly two miles north of uptown. Dogs are allowed. The county's parks
ordinance handles pets in a single sentence, Sec. 26: "It shall be unlawful to
have pets in the areas of County parks where pets are expressly prohibited."
Allowed by default, narrowed only where the county says otherwise.

Here, one place does say otherwise. The park holds one of the roughly twenty
community gardens the county runs, and the rules published for all of them read:
"No pets (only service animals are allowed. Please clean up after your service
animals; bag and remove off site.)" That language covers the garden plots. It
doesn't reach the rest of the grounds.

No leash rule is published for the park itself. County animal ordinance Sec.
3-71 requires physical restraint anywhere off your own property, which makes it
the operative rule out here. Water is the same story. Nothing published mentions
a fountain, and the county says nothing about bowls either way.

The county rebuilt this park and lists what came of it: walking trails, the
community gardens, multiple picnic shelters, public Wi-Fi, a playground,
indigenous wildflowers, and a picnic shelter that doubles as a stage with
restrooms attached. The basketball court is a full court with benches, paid for
by the Charlotte Hornets and Lowe's.

The county's own park record leaves the size field blank, so there is no
official acreage. Directories give it as seven acres. The record carries the
park as Developed, type Neighborhood, Central district, and the number on it,
(980) 314-1002, rings the Central Region parks office rather than anything on
site. Closing time is posted at the park and published nowhere online.

Anita Stroud Park sits about half a mile southwest, off Double Oaks Road.

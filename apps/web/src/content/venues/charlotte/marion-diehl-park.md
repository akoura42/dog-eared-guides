Marion Diehl Park is Mecklenburg County ground at 2219 Tyvola Road, and county
ownership settles the dog question. Section 26 of the Park and Recreation
Facilities Ordinance runs one sentence: "It shall be unlawful to have pets in
the areas of County parks where pets are expressly prohibited." Dogs are
allowed by default. Posted signage is the only thing that narrows that, and the
county keeps no page for this park, so nothing site-specific appears online
either way.

Leash rules for the grounds aren't published. County animal ordinance Sec. 3-71
requires physical restraint anywhere off your own property. The greenway is the
exception: the county does publish a rule there, and it reads "Dogs must be on
a 6ft or shorter leash and should be walked on the right edge of the trail."

That greenway cuts through the middle of the property. The county's own
description of the Briar–Little Hope segment, opened late 2024, has it starting
at Keystone Court, running southeast through Marion Diehl Park along Little
Hope Creek, and meeting the Little Sugar Creek Greenway and Cross Charlotte
Trail north of Tyvola Road. Paved, with the Tyvola Road trailhead at the park's
south edge. South of the junction, the Little Sugar runs 1.5 miles to
Huntingtowne Farms Park.

The east half is the Queens Sports Complex, a partnership between the county
and Queens University of Charlotte, sixty-five acres by the university's count.
Synthetic turf championship field for soccer and lacrosse, fenced and, in the
university's words, "locked when not in use to prevent damage." A quarter-mile
running track. An adaptive field built for people with disabilities, a field
house, a welcome center, and a 27-foot bronze lion named Rex.

Dirt singletrack loops the wooded western and southern edges. OpenStreetMap
tags three runs there, Marion Diehl, Farmbrook Loop and Tech Loop, as dirt,
one-way and bicycle-designated. Bikes arrive from one direction on those.

Little Hope Creek crosses the park, and Section 11 of the parks ordinance
covers it: no bathing dogs "in the streams, lakes, or other water features of
any park." Nothing published mentions drinking water.

The county record leaves acreage blank. Closing time is posted on site and
published nowhere online. The number in that record, (980) 314-1003, rings the
South Region parks office; the recreation center at the same address answers
separately at (980) 314-1300. Brawley's Beverage sits about half a mile north
on Park Road.

Hidden Valley Park is Mecklenburg County land, which settles the dog question.
The county's Park and Recreation Facilities Ordinance handles pets in one
sentence, Sec. 26: "It shall be unlawful to have pets in the areas of County
parks where pets are expressly prohibited." Allowed by default, then, with
posted signs the only thing that narrows it. The county has no page for this
park. Both plausible facility URLs return 404s, so nothing published online
names Hidden Valley either way.

The county's own record calls it undeveloped. Charlotte's parks GIS layer
carries it as a Neighborhood park, status "Undeveloped," in the North district,
addressed to 260 Northchase Drive. What's mapped on the ground doesn't quite
match that label. A paved path runs the length of the property, crossing the
water twice on concrete bridges, and a bench and a trash can sit beside it near
the middle. No agency publishes a name for that path.

The shape matters more than the acreage here. This isn't a block of grass. It's
a corridor, roughly four-tenths of a mile of it, running northwest from
Northchase Drive to Bilmark Avenue and following the water the whole way. The
channel is Little Sugar Creek, close enough to its headwaters that the
tributaries feeding in across the property are still unnamed. Two of them drop
into culverts.

Section 11 of the parks ordinance covers that water directly: no bathing dogs
"in the streams, lakes, or other water features of any park."

Leash rules for this park aren't published. County animal ordinance Sec. 3-71
requires physical restraint anywhere off your own property, which makes it the
operative rule on the grounds. Nothing published mentions water for dogs, and
no drinking fountain is mapped on the property.

Martin Luther King, Jr. Middle School sits just off the southwest edge at 500
Bilmark Avenue. Its ballfields and running track are school property, outside
the park boundary. The county leaves the size field blank in its record, so
there is no official acreage. The number in that record, (980) 314-1002, also
appears on county park records in other districts, which makes it a department
line rather than a phone on site.

Eastway Park is about three-quarters of a mile south, off Shamrock Drive.
Derita Park is roughly a mile and a half west.

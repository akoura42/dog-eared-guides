Elizabeth Park is a Mecklenburg County park, and the county's Park and
Recreation Facilities Ordinance is the document that governs dogs on it.
Section 26 runs one sentence: "It shall be unlawful to have pets in the areas
of County parks where pets are expressly prohibited." Dogs are allowed by
default. Posted signage is the only thing that narrows that, and the county
retired its per-park pages in the parkandrec.mecknc.gov redesign, so nothing
specific to Elizabeth Park appears online either way.

Mostly, the park is a block of the Little Sugar Creek Greenway. The paved trail
runs its length, Fourth Street at the south end to Elizabeth Avenue at the
north, a couple hundred yards. Little Sugar Creek runs open alongside it,
surfacing from a culvert at one end and dropping into another at the other.
Section 11 of the ordinance covers that water directly: no bathing dogs "in the
streams, lakes, or other water features of any park."

The park-wide leash rule isn't published. County animal ordinance Sec. 3-71
requires physical restraint anywhere off your own property, and Park and
Recreation's greenway guidance reaches the trail itself: "Dogs must be on a 6ft
or shorter leash and should be walked on the right edge of the trail." Water is
a blank. No fountain is mapped inside the property, and nothing published
mentions one.

North Kings Drive runs along one side the whole way. At the north end the
CityLYNX Gold Line crosses Elizabeth Avenue on street track, and the line's
CPCC stop sits on that stretch; streetcars come every thirty minutes, 6 a.m. to
11:26 p.m. on weekdays. Signalized crossings sit at both ends. Central
Piedmont's campus starts across Fourth Street.

On the ground: a bench, bike racks for twelve and for six, street lamps along
the southern walk. The Trail of History's Captain James Jack bronze, by Chas
Fagan, stands at the Fourth and Kings corner. Nothing published lists restrooms
or parking here.

The county's boundary record leaves the size field blank, so there's no
official acreage. Hours work the same way, by posting rather than publication.
And (980) 314-1002, the number in the county record, rings the Central Region
parks office rather than anything on site. Independence Park sits a little over
half a mile southeast on Hawthorne Lane; the greenway keeps north toward
Cordelia Park and south past Metropolitan.

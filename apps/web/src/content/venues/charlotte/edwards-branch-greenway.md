Edwards Branch Greenway is 13.4 acres of Mecklenburg County greenway property
with no trail on it. The county's greenway trails layer shows no built segment
here. Its master plan layer lists five Edwards Branch segments and gives every
one of them the development status "Future" and the trail status "Long Term."
Four of the five entered the plan in 1999.

The dog rule is settled anyway, because the land is county park property.
Section 26 of the Parks and Recreation Facilities Ordinance runs one sentence:
"It shall be unlawful to have pets in the areas of County parks where pets are
expressly prohibited." Dogs are allowed by default, and posted signage is the
only thing that narrows that. Nothing about a leash is published for this
property, which leaves county animal ordinance Sec. 3-71 as the operative rule,
requiring physical restraint anywhere off your own property. Section 11 of the
same parks ordinance bars bathing a dog in park streams. Edwards Branch runs the
length of the corridor.

The property comes in two pieces, roughly three quarters of a mile apart. Ten of
the acres sit at the west end off Coliseum Drive in Grier Heights, bought fee
simple in 1987 and added to in 2004, 2017 and 2018. The eastern three acres are
nine separate lots on Sheffield, Winfield and Dresden Drives, near Woodland
Drive, all acquired in May 2021, all recorded in county parcel data as
single-family residential, and all taken through Storm Water Services buyouts.
Floodplain land.

No trailhead and no parking. The county's greenway trail entrance layer returns
nothing near the corridor, and neither the county greenway list nor its current
greenway projects page names Edwards Branch at all.

The segments the master plan does describe, in its own wording: "Woodland Dr to
Sheffield Neighborhood Park" at 1.6 miles, "Briar Creek Greenway to Briar Creek
Rd," "Briar Creek Greenway to Independence Boulevard," and "E Independence Bv to
Commonwealth Av." A 2024 entry adds a 0.03-mile "Briar Creek & Edwards Branch
Connector."

Evergreen Nature Preserve, 77 acres of developed county land with built trails,
sits about a quarter mile northeast of the eastern lots. Sheffield Park, the
neighborhood park the master plan names as one terminus, is eight acres.

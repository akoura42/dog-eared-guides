PetSuites Charlotte Airport sits at 219 Archdale Drive in Montclaire South,
about five miles southeast of the terminal at Charlotte Douglas. Dogs are the
business here, so the policy question isn't whether they're allowed. It's what
gets them through the door.

Four vaccines for dogs: Rabies, DHPP, Bordetella, and Canine Influenza, all
"administered by a veterinary professional." Puppies need "at least the first
round of the 2 part Canine Influenza series." Minimum age is twelve weeks, and
dogs seven months and older have to be spayed or neutered to join group play.
The FAQ also names who gets turned away outright: guests in heat or coming into
heat, pregnant guests, and "guests with open wounds, stitches or contagious
diseases."

Breed isn't on that list. PetSuites says it caters "to all cats and dogs,
regardless of breed or special needs."

Group play isn't automatic either. "Each dog is given a two-part evaluation
before being permitted to join group play sessions," and dogs that don't clear
it, or don't want it, get one-on-one sessions with staff instead. The
evaluation day is free.

The yards run indoor and outdoor on what the site calls paw friendly turf, with
water features, raised beds scattered through for dogs that need a break, play
structures split by dog size, and "ample water bowls and overhangs to provide
shade." A Group Play Supervisor stays with each group.

Boarding splits into Classic and Luxury suites. Luxury adds raised beds, a
personal TV, and webcams. Both include room service, plush bedding, potty
walks, daily housekeeping and a Pawgress Report, with a 10 a.m. checkout.

Food comes from home, pre-bagged by meal. Staff administer medications at no
charge but can't give injections. Tours run without an appointment, and the
resort names 6:30 to 8 a.m. and 5 to 7 p.m. as its busiest stretches.

Hours are 6:30 a.m. to 7 p.m. Monday through Friday, 7 to 5 Saturday, and noon
to 5 Sunday. Nothing published covers leashing in the lobby or the parking lot,
which leaves the county animal ordinance as the operative rule.

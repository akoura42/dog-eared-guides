Kilborne Park is a Mecklenburg County park at 2600 Kilborne Drive, in east
Charlotte. County ownership is what sets the dog rule. Section 26 of the Park
and Recreation Facilities Ordinance makes it "unlawful to have pets in the
areas of County parks where pets are expressly prohibited," which leaves dogs
allowed by default across the grounds. Posted signage is the only thing that
narrows that, and the county's page for this park says nothing about dogs
either way.

Leash rules aren't published for the park. County animal ordinance Sec. 3-71
requires physical restraint anywhere off your own property, which makes it the
operative rule on the grounds. Nothing published mentions water for dogs.

Fifty-one acres, classified by the county as a Community park and marked
developed. The county's amenity list runs short and slightly odd: a 51-acre
field for sports like cricket and soccer, a disc golf course, the skate park, a
playground, a shelter, and greenway trail access, with no greenway named. The
county's project page for the site fills in the rest. Ball field, picnic
shelters, parking, restrooms, and the Kilborne DIY Skate Park.

The disc golf course covers a lot of the open ground. Charlotte Disc Golf Club
puts it at eighteen holes and dates the original installation to 1982, and the
county's stated design priorities for the coming renovation include minimizing
impact to disc golf.

Paths through the wooded sections are unpaved. OpenStreetMap tags them ground
and dirt rather than asphalt. A stream runs through the property, unnamed on
the map, and Section 11 of the parks ordinance covers it directly: no bathing
dogs "in the streams, lakes, or other water features of any park."

Construction is coming. Skate park work is scheduled for summer 2026 with the
Charlotte Skate Foundation, and main construction starts late 2026 — paved bike
and pedestrian connections out to Arnold Drive and Medallion Drive, expanded
parking, and a new picnic shelter near the playground.

Closing time is posted at the park and published nowhere online. The number in
the county record, (980) 314-1003, rings the South Region parks office rather
than anything on site.

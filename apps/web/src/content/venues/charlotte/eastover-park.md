Eastover Park is Mecklenburg County ground at 2730 Randolph Road, wrapped
around the back and side lawns of the Mint Museum Randolph. The county
publishes no page for the park itself. What governs dogs is the Parks and
Recreation Facilities Ordinance, which handles pets by exception. Sec. 26:
"It shall be unlawful to have pets in the areas of County parks where pets are
expressly prohibited." Nothing here is published as a prohibited area, so the
grounds are open to dogs by default, and a posted sign is the only thing that
would narrow it.

The leash rule for the park isn't published. The parks ordinance has no leash
section at all, which leaves county animal ordinance Sec. 3-71, requiring
physical restraint anywhere off your own property, as the operative rule on the
grass. The paved path is a separate case. It's a segment of the Briar Creek
Greenway, and the county's greenway rule runs "Dogs must be on a 6ft or shorter
leash and should be walked on the right edge of the trail." The county's
greenways list gives the segment as "1.0 miles between Randolph Road and
Meadowbrook Road."

Parking is the museum's. The county's own cleanup listings for this site say the
lot is "shared with the Mint Museum" and direct volunteers to "park at the end
close to the Museum for easy access to the Creek."

Randolph Road runs the north edge and crosses Briar Creek on a bridge there.
It's a secondary route, and the open floodplain near that end carries the
traffic noise with nothing to break it. The ground flattens and quiets as the
path drops toward the creek.

Briar Creek itself is the constant. Sec. 11 covers it directly: no bathing dogs
in "the streams, lakes, or other water features of any park." No drinking
fountain is mapped inside the property, and nothing published mentions one.

The county's park record leaves the size field blank, so there's no official
acreage. Directories give it as 19 acres and as more than 30. Closing time is
posted at the park and published nowhere online, and (980) 314-1002, the number
in the county record, rings the Central Region parks office rather than
anything on site.

Cherry Park sits about a mile and a half northwest. Freedom Park is a little
under two miles west, off the same side of town.

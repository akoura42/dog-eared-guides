Firestone Park is a Mecklenburg County park at 3501 Firestone Drive, in
northwest Charlotte. The county GIS parks layer records it as county-owned,
type "Community," status "Developed," and that ownership settles the dog
question. The Park and Recreation Facilities Ordinance covers every county
park, and Section 26 runs one sentence: "It shall be unlawful to have pets in
the areas of County parks where pets are expressly prohibited." Dogs are
allowed by default. A posted sign is the only thing that narrows that, and
Firestone has no page on parkandrec.mecknc.gov, so nothing park-specific is
documented online either way.

No leash rule is published for the park. The parks ordinance has no leash
section at all, which leaves county Animal Ordinance Sec. 3-71 as the operative
rule: physical restraint anywhere off your own property. Water is the same
story. No fountain is mapped on the property and nothing published mentions
one.

Creeks run through the western side, and the path network crosses them twice on
short footbridges. Section 11 of the parks ordinance speaks to that water
directly: "No person shall, in the streams, lakes, or other water features of
any park, bathe dogs or other animals, wash vehicles or clothing."

The paths change surface partway. Concrete near the open field and the
basketball court, gravel on the stretch running west toward the creek and the
bridges. The built cluster is small: one picnic shelter, a paved basketball
court with two hoops, and an open recreation field. A block of woods covers the
southeastern part of the property.

Milan Road borders the park on the east and north, with Dedmon Drive running
off it; both are residential streets, and no parking lot is mapped inside the
park boundary. The county's own park record leaves the size field blank, so
there is no official acreage. Directories give it as 55 acres and as 55.6.
Closing time is posted at the park and published nowhere online, and Section 31
makes it "unlawful for any person to fail to leave a park prior to the posted
closing time for that park." The number in the county record, (980) 314-1001,
rings the North Region parks office rather than anything on site.

Double Oaks Park sits about two and a half miles southeast.

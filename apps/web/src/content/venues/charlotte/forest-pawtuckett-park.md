Forest Pawtuckett Park is a Mecklenburg County neighborhood park at 1102
Ashford Street, west of uptown and a few blocks off Little Rock Road. The
county's parks ordinance is the document that governs dogs here, and Section 26
runs one sentence: "It shall be unlawful to have pets in the areas of County
parks where pets are expressly prohibited." Dogs are allowed by default. Posted
signage is the only thing that narrows that, and the county publishes no page
for this park, so nothing site-specific appears online either way. The
mecknc.gov address OpenStreetMap still links went dead in the redesign.

Leash rules for the park aren't published. County animal ordinance Sec. 3-71
requires physical restraint anywhere off your own property, which makes it the
operative rule on the grounds. Drinking water is the same story. No fountain is
mapped on the property, and nothing published mentions one.

A stream cuts through the site, and the path network crosses it on a
footbridge. Those paths are unpaved and short, signed for walkers and bikes
together with no separation between the two. Woods cover a good part of the
property, deciduous and evergreen mixed. Section 11 of the parks ordinance
reaches that water directly: no bathing dogs "in the streams, lakes, or other
water features of any park."

A picnic shelter is the only structure mapped inside the park. No parking lot
is mapped either, and Ashford Street, Eagle Peak Drive, and Crossridge Road are
all residential frontage, which leaves parking on-street. Little Rock Road runs
about a quarter mile east and carries the through traffic.

The county's park record leaves the size field blank, so there is no official
acreage. The closing time is posted at the park and published nowhere online,
and (980) 314-1001, the number in the county record, rings the North Region
parks office rather than anything on site.

Robert L. Smith Park, a developed county regional park, sits about half a mile
southeast at 1604 Little Rock Rd. Shuffletown Dog Park is roughly four miles
north, and under county rules a designated dog park is the only ground around
here where a dog is legally off leash.

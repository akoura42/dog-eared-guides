Little Sugar Creek Greenway is the spine of Charlotte's trail system: more
than seventeen paved miles, running from Cordelia Park north of Uptown,
south through Midtown, and on toward the South Carolina line as segments
finish. Mecklenburg County Park and Recreation runs it. The county's dog
rule is one sentence: "Dogs must be on a 6ft or shorter leash and should be
walked on the right edge of the trail."

The right-edge part carries weight here. This is a commuter and stroller
corridor, not a backcountry path. County etiquette puts pedestrians in the
right-of-way, asks cyclists to ride single file at a safe speed, and tells
everyone to keep right and pass on the left with an audible warning. In
congested stretches, users form a single line. The other posted rule about
dogs: pick up after them, and the bag goes in a trash can.

The best-known stretch is the urban mile and a quarter between East Seventh
Street and East Morehead Street, a hard-edged run of stone walls and
fountains beside the creek. Trail of History bronzes stand along it.
Captain James Jack at Fourth and Kings Drive, James B. Duke and Jane Wilkes
near Morehead and Kings, Thad Tate and William Henry Belk near Charlottetowne
and Metropolitan. Parking sits at the Metropolitan end and at Freedom Park,
where the greenway passes the Charlotte Nature Museum.

Construction is a constant on the Little Sugar. The county's closures page
currently carries a Midtown entry: scaffolding installed over the trail for
an adjacent development, a bicycle dismount zone beneath it, and a detour by
way of Kings Drive or Kenilworth Drive. That list changes segment by segment.

Two things the county doesn't publish: where the drinking fountains along the
corridor are, and any posted hours on its greenway pages. Dogs stay leashed
everywhere in Mecklenburg County parks outside the designated dog parks,
which are the only legal off-leash ground in the system.

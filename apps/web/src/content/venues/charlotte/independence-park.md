Independence Park sits at the western edge of Elizabeth, on both sides of
Hawthorne Lane. Mecklenburg County owns and runs it, which the Charlotte GIS
parks layer confirms: a Central-region neighborhood park, leased to the county,
in 28204. So the county parks ordinance governs dogs here, and Section 26 is
the whole of it: "It shall be unlawful to have pets in the areas of County
parks where pets are expressly prohibited." Allowed by default. A posted sign
is the only thing that narrows that, and the county publishes no page for this
park, so nothing site-specific appears online either way.

Leash rules for the park aren't published. County animal ordinance Sec. 3-71
requires physical restraint anywhere off your own property, which makes it the
operative rule on the grounds. Nothing published mentions water bowls.

Hawthorne Lane splits the place, and the two halves read differently. Below the
lane: a winding path, a rose garden, a reflecting pond, a gazebo, mature shade
trees. Above it: the playground, the athletic fields, and the 300-seat
Independence Park Stadium. Both sides sit in a bowl below the roadway, so
traffic passes above the tree line rather than alongside the paths.

A nearly $6 million county renovation reopened the park in 2023. It brought
permanent restrooms, a nature playground, a reconfigured parking lot off Park
Drive, and a twelve-foot-wide greenway running out to Hawthorne Lane and on to
Little Sugar Creek Greenway. That connector is greenway rather than park
ground, and the county's posted greenway rule is tighter than the park's: a
six-foot-or-shorter leash, dog walked on the right edge of the trail.

The pond has its own line in the ordinance. Section 11 bars bathing dogs "in
the streams, lakes, or other water features of any park."

Closing time is posted at the park and published nowhere online. The number in
the county record, (980) 314-1002, rings the Central Region parks office rather
than anything on site. Elizabeth Park is about half a mile west, on Elizabeth
Avenue.

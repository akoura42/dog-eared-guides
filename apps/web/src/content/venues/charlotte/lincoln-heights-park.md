Lincoln Heights Park sits at 1819 Catherine Simmons Avenue, in the neighborhood
it shares a name with. Mecklenburg County owns and runs it, so the county's
parks ordinance is the document that governs dogs. Section 26 is one sentence:
"It shall be unlawful to have pets in the areas of County parks where pets are
expressly prohibited." Dogs are allowed by default across the grounds, and an
express prohibition is the only thing that narrows that.

Here, one area carries that prohibition. The county's Community Gardens program
lists this park as a garden site, and the program rules read "No pets (only
service animals are allowed. Please clean up after your service animals)." The
plots occupy a block between the parking lot and the basketball court. The rule
reaches the garden, not the rest of the park.

Leash requirements for the park aren't published. County animal ordinance
Sec. 3-71 requires physical restraint anywhere off your own property, which
makes it the operative rule on the grounds. Nothing published mentions water
bowls either way. The National Recreation and Park Association, which funded
the 2014 rebuild, describes a drinking fountain at the basketball half-court.

That rebuild put a paved walking loop of about half a mile through the site,
replacing the gravel path from the original 2011 build. One asphalt lot opens
off Catherine Simmons. Playground equipment sits in two sections at the west
end, with a picnic shelter and a weather shelter a few steps apart beside them,
and there's a single-hoop basketball court with no lights, an open field, and
five outdoor fitness stations. A strip of deciduous woods runs along the
southern edge.

The county record leaves the size field blank, so there's no official acreage;
NRPA gives it as nine acres. Closing time is posted at the park and published
nowhere online. The number in the county record, (980) 314-1002, rings the
Central Region parks office rather than anything on site. Double Oaks Park is
about six-tenths of a mile southeast, and Druid Hills Park about a mile east.

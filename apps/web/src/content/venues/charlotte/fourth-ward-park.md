Fourth Ward Park is a Mecklenburg County park, and the county's Park and
Recreation Facilities Ordinance is the document that governs dogs here. Section
26 runs one sentence: "It shall be unlawful to have pets in the areas of County
parks where pets are expressly prohibited." Dogs are allowed by default. Posted
signage is the only thing that narrows that, and the county retired its per-park
pages in the parkandrec.mecknc.gov redesign, so nothing specific to Fourth Ward
appears online either way.

No leash rule is published for the park. County animal ordinance Sec. 3-71
requires physical restraint anywhere off your own property, which makes it the
operative rule on the grounds. Park and Recreation does publish a six-foot leash
length, but that language sits in the department's greenway guidance and is
scoped to greenways and trails. This isn't one. Off-leash ground in Mecklenburg
County means a designated dog park, and the nearest is Ninth Street Dog Park,
roughly three blocks north in the same neighborhood, open 7:30 a.m. to sunset.

The fountains come with a rule attached. Section 11 of the parks ordinance bars
bathing dogs "in the streams, lakes, or other water features of any park."

Three acres, between two entrances: Sixth and Poplar at one end, Seventh and
Pine at the other. Walking paths run between them, past a children's play
structure and planting beds that the Fourth Ward Neighborhood Association tends
alongside county staff on scheduled workdays. The association's own list of
what's in those beds runs to flowering apricot, serviceberry, Japanese maple,
edgeworthia, and several hellebores. Ceramic birdhouses by Joan Bankemper, a
former McColl Artist in Residence, are set through the plantings.

Water for dogs isn't published one way or the other. Neither is the closing
time, which Sec. 31 makes enforceable but which appears only on the sign at the
park. The county's own park record leaves the size field blank, so the
three-acre figure traces to Center City Partners and the neighborhood
association rather than to the county. The number in the county record, (980)
314-1002, rings the Central Region parks office rather than anything on site.

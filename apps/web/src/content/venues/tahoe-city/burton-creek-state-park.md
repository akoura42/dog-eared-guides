Burton Creek is the undeveloped state park on the forested slope northeast
of Tahoe City. Six miles of unpaved roadway run through it, open to hiking,
mountain biking, and cross-country skiing, sunrise to sunset. No motorized
vehicles. California State Parks puts the rest plainly: "Water, restrooms,
and other services are not available."

The dog rule is narrow, and the exact wording matters. The park page reads
"Dogs allowed only on fire roads." Those six miles of unpaved roadway are
the fire roads, and they are the full extent of where a dog is permitted
inside the boundary. Singletrack, meadow, and creek bank are not included.
The statewide State Parks pet rule says the same thing at more length:
dogs on fire roads, paved roads, campgrounds and day-use picnic areas, and
not on trails, not in open forest or meadows, not near creeks. Leashes cap
at six feet.

Winter narrows it further. From November 1 to May 1, when snow is present,
dogs are off designated ski trails throughout the Sierra District, to
protect the groomed Nordic courses. Those groomed courses run here: the
state park's own page hands off winter questions to Tahoe XC, whose trail
system winds through Burton Creek, Antone Meadows, and neighboring National
Forest and California Tahoe Conservancy land. Tahoe XC grooms its own
dog-specific trails under its own rules, which are a separate matter from
the state park's.

What the official page does not give is a way in. It lists no street
address, no trailhead, and no parking area, and its directions line reads,
in full, "Burton Creek State Park is located on the northeast side of Tahoe
City." The map is a 2008 PDF. Nothing in the state park's material
describes shade, water access, or where the fire roads begin.

Silver Creek sits in the Truckee River canyon on Highway 89, six miles south
of Truckee, with the highway along one side and the river along the other.
The Forest Service page puts the trade-off in one line: "Highway 89 road noise
can be heard."

The dog rule is specific and comes from the campground's own reservation
notices. "6 people and 2 dogs maximum per site. Dogs shall always be kept on
leash no longer than six feet or otherwise confined. Violation notices WILL be
issued for non-compliance." Two dogs. Six feet. A stated willingness to write
it up. No pet fee appears anywhere in the listing.

Nineteen single-family sites, seven of them tent-only and walk-in, a short
carry from the parking area and overlooking the river. Every site has a picnic
table, a campfire ring with a grill, and a bear box. The bear box is
mandatory, and the Forest Service names pet food specifically among the
scented items that go in it.

The two official sources contradict each other on water. The Tahoe National
Forest page states "Potable water is not available at this site." The
Recreation.gov listing for the same campground says vault toilets and drinking
water are provided. Vault toilets are on both. The water question is
unresolved in print, and the Truckee Ranger District number above is the way
to settle it.

Twenty dollars a night for a single site, five dollars per night for each
additional vehicle. Sites reserve through Recreation.gov; first-come sites
take cash only, because there's no card reader on site. RVs longer than 24
feet are restricted by the turnaround space. Check-in is 2 p.m., check-out
noon, quiet hours 10 p.m. to 6 a.m.

The river draws fly-fishers for rainbow trout, and swimming and tubing in the
warm months. Nothing in the campground's rules addresses dogs in the water,
and the six-foot leash requirement is written without exception.

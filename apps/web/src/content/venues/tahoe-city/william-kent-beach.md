Dogs are not allowed on the sand at William Kent. The Forest Service names the
beach directly on its Lake Tahoe dog page, listing it with Nevada, Pope,
Baldwin, Meeks Bay and Zephyr Cove as a designated swimming beach closed to
pets. The site's own page says it plainly: "No pets allowed on the beach."

The picnic area is a different matter. The Lake Tahoe Basin Management Unit's
baseline reads that a well-behaved, leashed dog is welcome almost anywhere in
the unit, with designated swimming beaches named as the exception. Leashed dogs
are covered on the picnic grounds, then. Not on the beach below them. No
published rule marks where the picnic area stops and the swimming beach starts.

The day-use site sits on the lake side of Highway 89 about two miles south of
Tahoe City, directly across the road from William Kent Campground. Picnic
tables under the pines. Restrooms are open in season and shut for the winter,
along with trash service, though the area itself stays open year-round.
Campfires are out entirely — wood, charcoal, and other solid fuels are all
prohibited — and portable stoves with on/off valves require a valid permit.

Leashed dogs are allowed across the highway at the campground, where the Forest
Service caps it at two pets per site and repeats the same no-pets-on-beaches
line. The 64-Acres trailhead is a couple of miles north, at the head of TCPUD's
paved trail network, which takes leashed dogs. Kaspian's day-use area lies
farther down the West Shore.

The Forest Service page doesn't mention water bowls, shade structures, or a
day-use fee one way or the other.

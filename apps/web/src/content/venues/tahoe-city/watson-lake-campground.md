Watson Lake Campground sits at 7,759 feet off Forest Road 16N50, a
backcountry site the Forest Service describes in exactly those terms. The
Lake Tahoe Basin Management Unit runs it directly, without a concessionaire.
No reservations, no fee.

The dog rule here is the agency's, not the campground's. LTBMU publishes one
baseline for its own lands: "Your well-behaved, leashed dog is welcome almost
anywhere within the Lake Tahoe Basin Management Unit, with the notable
exceptions of designated swimming beaches and areas that are restricted for
wildlife protection." Leash on, pick up after them, with a 6-foot leash named
on the agency's packing list. A separate line speaks to campers: don't leave
dogs unattended at the campsite. Nothing specific to Watson Lake modifies any
of it, and no pet fee or per-site dog limit appears anywhere — unlike Kaspian
Campground down on the West Shore, whose reservation notices cap it at two
pets and close the beaches to them.

Six sites, first come, first served, five days per season. That cap is
unusual. Forest Order No. 19-24-05 gives most basin campgrounds fourteen days
a year and names Watson Lake separately at five, with camping outside the
designated spots prohibited under the same order. A vault restroom, unserviced
in winter. No trash pickup, so everything carried in comes back out.

No potable water at the site. The lake is the water, and the Tahoe Rim Trail
Association's guidance for this stretch is that it has to be treated first.
The TRT passes the lake on its 20.2-mile Tahoe City to Brockway Summit
segment; TRTA describes a shallow lake thickly fringed with wildflowers in
spring and early summer. Campfires are legal in the iron rings here, one of
seventeen sites in Exhibit A of the basin campfire order. Along the Rim Trail
itself they aren't.

One live condition: LTBMU has an active plague alert for the basin. Its pet
guidance is to keep them confined or leashed, away from rodent burrows and
dead rodents, and treated with flea control.

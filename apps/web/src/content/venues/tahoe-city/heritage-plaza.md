Dogs are not allowed at Heritage Plaza. The plaza is a Placer County park,
and the county's Public Recreation Areas code, Article 12.24, is blunt about
the basin: "No dogs (excepting assistance dogs) or horses are permitted
within the boundaries of any PRA in the Lake Tahoe Basin, unless specifically
authorized by the enforcement official." Assistance dogs are the written
exception. Nothing on the county's page for this park authorizes anything
else. The same article sets the hours the county publishes here, open half an
hour before sunrise and closed half an hour after sunset, which is one more
sign the plaza is administered under it.

That puts Heritage Plaza in the same column as Commons Beach, three blocks
west, and Lake Forest Beach out past the boat ramp. County land, county rule,
no dogs.

The plaza is small and easy to miss from the road: 0.8 acres of lakefront
squeezed between North Lake Boulevard and the water, the last undeveloped
parcel in the commercial core until the county bought it with a California
Tahoe Conservancy grant. Stairs and a ramp drop from the sidewalk to a paved
terrace with seat walls, benches, picnic tables, and interpretive panels on
Tahoe City history. The thing people stop for is Ursa Mater, a nearly
thirteen-foot black bear and two cubs assembled from more than 203,000
donated pennies. Watson Cabin, the oldest log building on the North Shore,
sits next door.

The paved lakeside trail runs along the plaza's upper edge, and TCPUD's trail
rule takes leashed dogs on that pavement. The boundary is the terrace: the
trail is trail, and the plaza below it is a county PRA. No official source
extends the trail allowance down the stairs or to the shoreline. The fenced
off-leash park on Grove Street, a few blocks inland, is the nearest county-
adjacent place in town where dogs are the point.

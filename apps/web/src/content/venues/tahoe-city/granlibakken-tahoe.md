Granlibakken does not allow dogs. The resort's FAQ puts it plainly: "For the
health and convenience of other guests and to help us provide the cleanest and
most sanitary accommodations available, pets are not permitted." A $250
cleaning fee, before taxes, applies to any room found to have had a pet in it.
The one exception is ADA service animals individually trained to perform tasks
for a person with a disability. The FAQ names emotional support and therapy
animals as not qualifying.

The policy is written for the resort rather than broken out by area. Nothing on
the site sets a separate rule for the grounds, the ski and sled hill, or the
Tahoe Treetop Adventure Park, and nothing on the site relaxes the ban for them
either.

Worth knowing because the setting reads like dog country and isn't. The
property sits about a mile inland from the lake and the Truckee River, up a
side valley at the end of Granlibakken Road, off Highway 89 a short drive south
of the Y. Lodging runs from studios to three-bedroom townhomes scattered among
the pines, with the hill rising behind them. Parking is on site and included.

Two dog-legal things sit at the property's edge, and the FAQ points guests at
both. A partially paved fire road drops from the resort down to the Truckee
River. And the Dianne Feinstein West Shore Trail, a class 1 multi-use path,
picks up where Granlibakken Road meets Highway 89. That trail belongs to
TCPUD's 23-mile paved network, where the posted rule is "keep dogs on a short
leash." The 64-Acres trailhead, with the network's largest lot and restrooms,
is a mile and a half north.

For an overnight in the same stretch of town, Basecamp Tahoe City publishes a
$40 one-time pet fee and allows dogs in select rooms and throughout its common
areas.

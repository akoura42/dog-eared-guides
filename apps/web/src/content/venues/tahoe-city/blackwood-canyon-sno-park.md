Blackwood Canyon SNO-PARK is a parking area on the west side of Highway 89,
three miles south of Tahoe City, where Barker Pass Road leaves the highway and
climbs west into the canyon. California State Parks runs the permit program.
The ground itself is National Forest under the Lake Tahoe Basin Management
Unit, and the LTBMU's dog rule is the one that applies: leashed dogs are
welcome almost anywhere in the unit, with designated swimming beaches and
wildlife-closure areas as the exceptions. Blackwood Canyon is on neither list.

The permit is the thing that separates this from a pullout. From November 1
through May 30 every parked vehicle needs one, $15 for a day or $40 for the
season, on the left side of the dashboard. Parking is first-come, first-served,
and there's no refund if the lot is full. Overnight camping is prohibited here
under Forest Order 19-24-05.

Facilities in winter: none. No restrooms, no water, no trash service at the
LTBMU's west-shore sites once the season ends, so dog waste comes back out with
you. The state's page lists cross-country skiing, dog sledding, and snow play as
the activities here. It doesn't say whether snowmobiles use Barker Pass Road,
though Placer County's visitor site describes the road as a route for both
snowmobilers and cross-country skiers.

The road is drivable in the warm months. Blackwood Canyon Campground sits about
three miles up it, six sites with fire rings and a restroom, open May 22 to
October 13 in 2026, with no potable water. Same leash rule the whole way.

Kaspian Day Use Area is a few minutes north on 89. William Kent Beach, a couple
of miles further toward town, is one of the seven designated swim beaches where
the LTBMU prohibits pets outright.

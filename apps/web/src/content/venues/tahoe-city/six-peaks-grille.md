Dogs aren't allowed at Six Peaks Grille. The restaurant sits inside Everline
Resort & Spa, and Everline's Know Before You Go page states it plainly:
"Family pets are not permitted. Bringing a pet to the Resort will result in a
room recovery fee." That prohibition is written against the Resort as a whole.
Nothing on the site carves out the dining rooms, the terrace, or the grounds,
and none of the resort's other restaurants and bars carries pet language of
its own.

The amount is published in the same section. "You will be charged a room
recovery fee in the amount of $250 per pet and you will be asked to take your
pet to a local, third party boarding facility." The resort doesn't name the
facility. Service animals are the one exception, worded narrowly: Everline
"makes accommodations for service animals that perform work or tasks as
defined by the American Disabilities Act (ADA)." The same page adds that
fraudulently misrepresenting an animal as a service animal, or as a service
animal in training, is unlawful.

The terrace is real. Wooden tables, string lights, a stone fire pit, and the
valley's six named peaks straight ahead. It reads as exactly the kind of patio
that takes dogs. It doesn't.

This is the resort's steakhouse, open Wednesday through Sunday from 5 to 9 pm,
with hours the site says may vary by season and occupancy. It's at the end of
Resort Road at the head of Olympic Valley, past the golf course, off Highway
89 north of Tahoe City. The property spent decades as Resort at Squaw Creek,
and older listings still file the restaurant under that name.

For dinner with a dog in the same valley, PlumpJack Cafe and Bar is about a
mile and a half west on Olympic Valley Road. Its patio is reported to take
leashed dogs, on the strength of Palisades Tahoe's pet page and visitor
reports rather than anything the cafe publishes itself. Patio lunch there runs
Saturday and Sunday only, noon to 3 pm, in summer.

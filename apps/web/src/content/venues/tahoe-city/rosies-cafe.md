Rosie's doesn't publish a dog policy anywhere. Not the website, not the
Facebook page, not Instagram. What's on this page comes from visitor reports
and directory listings, which agree on one thing: dogs are described as
welcome on the outdoor deck, and only there. Nothing found addresses the
dining room.

The tahoe.com listing puts it as "when open, the patio at Rosie's Cafe is
dog-friendly." A Tripadvisor review sits under the heading "Dog Friendly
Deck," and a North Tahoe realty roundup describes the same deck. That "when
open" carries weight. None of the three sources gives a schedule for deck
seating, and Rosie's doesn't post one.

Leash rules aren't published either, so Placer County's leash law is the
operative rule. Water bowls, size limits, fees: no source addresses any of
them, and they stay blank here rather than get guessed at.

The building sits at 571 North Lake Boulevard, a couple of blocks up from
Commons Beach, where dogs aren't allowed on the sand at all.

Inside, the room is a collection. Deer and elk chandeliers salvaged from the
Tahoe Tavern, which stood from 1901 to 1964. A slab of the "Big Tree," the
120-year-old Jefferson pine that was Tahoe City's landmark until it came down
in 1994. Antique bicycles hang overhead, a 1951 Black Phantom Whizzer among
them. The building opened as a grocery store and has served food since 1964,
under the current owner since 1989.

Kitchen hours run 9 to 9 Monday, 9 to 4 Tuesday, 9 to 9 Wednesday through
Friday, and 8 to 9 Saturday and Sunday. The bar stays open until 11 daily.
The menu on the official site is posted as eight scanned page images rather
than text, so no dish list is captured here.

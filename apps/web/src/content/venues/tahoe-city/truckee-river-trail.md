If you do one walk with your dog in Tahoe City, it's this one. The Truckee
River Trail follows the river downstream from town toward Olympic Valley,
paved the whole way, with the water in sight or earshot for most of it. It's
one of three legs of TCPUD's 23-mile multi-use network — the West Shore and
North Shore trails are the others — and all of it takes dogs under the same
rule: short leash, always.

Start at 64-Acres Park, the primary trailhead, just south of the Highway 89/28
"Y" at Granlibakken Road. There's real parking here (rare for Tahoe
trailheads), plus restrooms and picnic areas; water fountains appear along
the network. From 64-Acres the river leg runs flat and easy — this is a
walk-as-far-as-you-like trail rather than a summit mission, which makes it
ideal for older dogs, hot days, or the first morning at altitude.

Know what you're sharing the pavement with: bikes, lots of them, with a 15
mph speed limit and Class 1 and 2 e-bikes permitted. The short-leash rule
exists because of exactly this traffic. Keep your dog on your right, off the
center line, and expect fast passes on summer weekends. Early mornings are
dramatically calmer.

Two seasonal notes. In winter, TCPUD plows the network on a prioritized
schedule once three inches fall, so the paved trail is the town's most
reliable snow-season dog walk — a genuine rarity in the basin, where most
dog-legal routes disappear under snow (and the state-park ski trails ban dogs
outright from November through May). And on the West Shore leg,
reconstruction work has been closing sections with detours through
neighborhood streets; check TCPUD's trail page before committing to a long
one-way walk. River access points along the Truckee leg let a hot dog get
wet — mind the current in early summer.

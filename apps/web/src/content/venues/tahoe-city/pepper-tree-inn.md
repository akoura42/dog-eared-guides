Pepper Tree Inn is the seven-story building on North Lake Boulevard, and it's
the tallest thing in downtown Tahoe City. The hotel's own page dates the
structure to construction ahead of the 1960 Winter Olympics. Rooms look across
the boulevard at the lake. There's on-site parking.

Dogs stay on the ground floor. That is the entire published shape of the
policy: "Pets are permitted in ground floor rooms only," which appears on the
standard rooms page and nowhere else on the site — the deluxe and superior
room pages carry no pet language at all. Booking is the other half of it.
"Pet friendly rooms are only available by phone," the page says, followed by
"Call us to avoid any additional charges." The online booking widget doesn't
handle it.

What the inn doesn't publish is the size of those charges. No fee figure
appears anywhere on the site. No leash rule either, so county leash law
applies on the grounds. Nothing about water bowls, size limits, or breeds.
Service animals sit outside all of this: the accessibility page says the inn
fully welcomes all service animals under ADA requirements, which is not a pet
policy and carries no fee.

The address does specific things. Commons Beach is a few blocks down the
boulevard toward the Y, and dogs aren't allowed on the beach itself. The paved
lakefront trail that runs past it does take leashed dogs, and it's a walk from
the front door. The Tahoe Tap Haus patio sits along the same stretch. 64-Acres
Park, the trailhead with actual parking, is about five minutes by car.

Third-party booking sites disagree with each other about this hotel's pet
rules, some listing a flat pet fee and others saying no pets at all. The phone
number above is the only place the fee gets settled.

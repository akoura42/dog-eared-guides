Dogs don't sleep here. Palisades Tahoe's pet policy says it directly: "While
non-service pets are not allowed to stay in rooms at The Village at Palisades
Tahoe, dogs are welcome to lodge at PlumpJack Inn or at various Airbnb or VRBO
rentals in the Olympic Valley or Alpine Meadows area." Service animals are the
carve-out. The same page states they "are allowed into all areas of the
facility where customers are normally allowed to go."

The grounds run on a different rule than the rooms. "Leashed dogs are
permitted in The Village at Palisades Tahoe and our parking lots year-round,"
and the policy restates it with the operative verb: dogs on leash "are allowed
to walk through The Village at Palisades Tahoe and our parking lots
year-round with their owners." Walk through, not check in. That covers the
pedestrian plaza between the buildings, the paths out to the lifts, and the
heated underground parking.

The Village itself is the cluster of suite-style condo buildings at 1750
Village East Road: studios through three-bedrooms, a front desk staffed around
the clock, eight outdoor hot tubs. In winter the resort's ban extends past the
buildings to the mountain, since "pets are not permitted on the ski runs or
chairlifts at Palisades Tahoe during the winter season." Summer opens the
valley back up. Leashed dogs are allowed on designated hiking trails, at most
outdoor Village events, and in the water at Cushing Pond, which the resort
places "behind KT Base Bar in The Village."

The resort names its own alternative a few hundred feet away. PlumpJack Inn,
"conveniently located across from the Tram Building," takes dogs and puts an
in-room bed, food and water bowls, treats, and cleanup bags in the room; a
one-time cleaning fee per dog is charged at check-in, and the amount isn't
published. Everline Resort & Spa, further down the valley on Resort Road, is a
verified no. Fifteen minutes down SR 89 in Tahoe City, Mother Nature's Inn
takes dogs indoors for $75 per stay, and Basecamp Tahoe City for $40 once per
visit.

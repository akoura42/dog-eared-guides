Dogs aren't allowed at Iron Road Noodle House, because dogs aren't allowed on
the property it sits on. Everline Resort & Spa's Know Before You Go page puts
it flatly: "Family pets are not permitted. Bringing a pet to the Resort will
result in a room recovery fee." That fee is $250 per pet, and the same passage
says the pet then goes to "a local, third party boarding facility."

The ban is written against "the Resort," with no carve-out for the grounds or
the on-site restaurants, and Iron Road is one of those restaurants. Everline's
own announcement places it "just a few steps away from Resort Chair," the lift
at the base of the resort's ski terrain. Service animals are the one published
exception, worded to the ADA definition of animals that perform work or tasks.
The resort adds that misrepresenting an animal as a service animal is unlawful.

It's a winter operation. CoralTree Hospitality, which runs Everline, listed the
noodle house as closed for the season in August 2026, and the resort's opening
posts have landed in December. The food, per the resort's opening announcement
covered in the Sierra Sun: three ramen bowls, chashu buns, pork wontons,
edamame, a short sake and cocktail list. The name refers to the roughly 20,000
Chinese immigrants who built the Central Pacific Railroad through the Sierra in
the 1860s. Neither Everline nor CoralTree publishes a readable menu page for
the outlet, so nothing is captured in this listing's menu data.

The nearest alternative is about a mile west at the Palisades base, where
PlumpJack Cafe and Bar takes dogs on its patio, though that rests on visitor
reports rather than anything the venue publishes. A few miles south on Highway
89, at the mouth of Alpine Meadows Road, River Ranch Lodge allows leashed dogs
on its deck over the Truckee River and says so on its own site. Both are a
drive from here. Resort Road runs about a mile in from the highway.

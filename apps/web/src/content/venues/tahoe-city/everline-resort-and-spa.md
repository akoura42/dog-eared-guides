Dogs aren't allowed at Everline Resort & Spa. The resort's Know Before You Go
page states it plainly: "Family pets are not permitted. Bringing a pet to the
Resort will result in a room recovery fee." The amount is published in the
same section. "You will be charged a room recovery fee in the amount of $250
per pet and you will be asked to take your pet to a local, third party
boarding facility." That $250 isn't a pet fee that buys a dog a night. It's
what a dog on the property costs, and the dog still leaves.

The ban is written property-wide. Nothing on the site carves out the grounds,
the golf course, the meadow, or the pool deck, and the four on-site
restaurants get no separate pet language of their own. Service animals are
the one exception, worded narrowly: Everline "makes accommodations for
service animals that perform work or tasks as defined by the American
Disabilities Act (ADA)." The same page adds that fraudulently misrepresenting
an animal as a service animal, or as a service animal in training, is
unlawful.

This is the property that spent decades as Resort at Squaw Creek, and plenty
of older listings and booking pages still file it under that name. The policy
reads the same under both. It sits at 400 Resort Road at the head of Olympic
Valley, off Highway 89 north of Tahoe City. The golf course and the meadow
start at the building's edge, and the Palisades Tahoe runs rise behind it.

Everline doesn't name the boarding facility it would send a dog to. Down
Highway 89 in Tahoe City, Basecamp allows dogs in select rooms and common
areas for a flat $40 per visit, and the paved lakefront and river trails
there take leashed dogs.

California State Parks bans dogs from beaches as a baseline rule, which is
why this small day-use area matters far beyond its size: it's the posted
exception. At the spot where Lake Tahoe empties into the Truckee River —
next to the historic dam, the Gatekeeper's Museum, and Fanny Bridge — the
Sierra District's dog order carves out the "Gatekeepers" day-use shoreline
as legal ground for leashed dogs.

Read the rule the way a ranger would: dogs allowed *only* in this day-use
area along the shoreline, leashed at all times, never unattended. Walk east
into the main Tahoe State Recreation Area and the exception ends — the
campground beach a quarter mile away is a no-dog zone, full stop.

What you actually get here is one of the most interesting corners of Tahoe
City. The lake's only outlet pours through the dam gates beside you, Fanny
Bridge (yes, that's its real name — people lean over the rail to watch the
trout, fannies to the road) is steps away, and the shoreline gives you real
Lake Tahoe water access in the middle of town. For a dog, it's the closest
legal "toes in the lake" moment to downtown — Commons Beach, five minutes
east, bans dogs entirely.

Practical notes: State Parks doesn't publish day-use hours or fees for this
spot, and the exact boundary of the dog-allowed zone isn't mapped online —
stay near the outlet/museum side and you're within it. Parking clusters
around the Fanny Bridge area and fills on summer weekends; coming on foot
via the paved trail from 64-Acres Park (leashed dogs welcome the whole way)
is often faster than circling for a spot. Combine with the Gatekeeper's
Museum grounds and the trail and you've got a solid two-hour downtown loop
that never breaks a rule.

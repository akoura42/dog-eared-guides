California State Parks bans dogs from its beaches as a baseline rule.
This small day-use area is the posted exception. At the spot where Lake
Tahoe empties into the Truckee River, beside the historic dam and the
Gatekeeper's Museum, the Sierra District's dog order carves out the
"Gatekeepers" day-use shoreline as legal ground for leashed dogs.

The rule is narrow. Dogs are allowed only in this day-use area along the
shoreline, leashed at all times, never unattended. The exception ends at
the edge of the main Tahoe State Recreation Area to the east — the
campground beach a quarter mile away is a no-dog zone.

The corner itself is one of the more interesting ones in Tahoe City. The
lake's only outlet pours through the dam gates, and Fanny Bridge is steps
away — the real name; people lean over the rail to watch the trout,
fannies to the road. This shoreline is the closest legal lake access to
downtown for a dog. Commons Beach, five minutes east, bans dogs entirely.

State Parks doesn't publish day-use hours or fees for this spot, and the
exact boundary of the dog-allowed zone isn't mapped online; the shoreline
near the outlet and museum sits inside it. Parking clusters around the
Fanny Bridge area and fills on summer weekends. The paved trail from
64-Acres Park reaches the area on foot, with leashed dogs allowed the
whole way, and the Gatekeeper's Museum grounds sit alongside.
